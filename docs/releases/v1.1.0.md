**Octavio** 1.1 is a big step towards the sound of a real violin, with a new baroque violin, two bowed guitars and 28 new presets.

## A more real violin

- **Notes ring on after you let go.** The bow now leaves the string still moving, as a player's does, so each note blooms into the room instead of stopping dead. The strings also ring twice as long.
- **Less like a synth lead.** Slurs change finger instead of gliding, and only a shift of the hand slides. The bow arm wanders a little and the left hand has a faint tremor, so held notes are never perfectly still. Every note lands a touch flat and settles, and the vibrato blooms into each note.
- **A richer, more uneven body.** Real violins are much less even from one harmonic to the next than the measured bodies alone, and the top octave above 10 kHz was missing. Both are back, so under vibrato each harmonic swells and fades on its own.
- **A sense of place.** A few early reflections sit between the violin and the Room, so it sounds played in a space even with very little Room. Room at 0 is still completely dry, for your own reverb.
- **Cleaner note changes.** Fingerings stay in first position where a player would, and string crossings and hidden shifts are smoother.

These were tuned against Dmitry Sinkovsky's recording of Biber's Passacaglia; see `docs/REFERENCE_SOUND.md` for the measurements.

## New instruments

- **Baroque violin**, a fourth choice under Instrument: gut strings that ring on longer, open strings that answer every note, and a bow that lifts off at the end of each stroke. Try the **Baroque Violin** preset. It plays at A440; transpose a semitone down in your DAW for baroque pitch (A415).
- **Bowed electric guitar** and **bowed acoustic guitar**: a guitar played with a violin bow, with pickup, drive and drone controls for the electric and drones for the acoustic.

## New controls

- **Bow Noise** sets the grain of rosin and hair in the sound. 50% matches real held notes; 0 is the clean model.
- **Intonation** sets how far each note strays from true pitch. 10% is a modern soloist; about 30% is historically informed playing.

Both can be automated.

## Install or update

| Platform | File | Install to |
|---|---|---|
| Windows | `Octavio-VST3-Windows.zip` | `C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\` |
| macOS (Apple silicon and Intel) | `Octavio-VST3-macOS.zip`, `Octavio-AU-macOS.zip` | `~/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST3/` or `.../Components/` |
| Linux | `Octavio-VST3-Linux.zip` | `~/.vst3/` |

**Updating from 1.0:** close your DAW, then unzip and copy `Octavio.vst3` over the old one (replace it when asked). Your projects and presets carry over, and there is no need to rescan. Projects saved with 1.0 open with the new sound; their settings are unchanged.

**New install:** unzip, copy to the folder above, then rescan plugins in your DAW and add **Octavio** (vendor OctahedronV2). If you still have the pre-1.0 `Violin Synthesizer.vst3`, delete it first. The macOS builds are not notarised; see the README for the one-line `xattr` command that lets them load.
